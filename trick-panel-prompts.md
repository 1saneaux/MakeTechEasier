# Trick Storyboard — Image Prompts

Prompts for regenerating `assets/trick-1.jpg` … `trick-5.jpg`. They keep the current
art style. Each attempt changes **one variable** (foot placement, flick, or pop), and
each failed attempt ends with the skater falling or missing the board.

Save the outputs with the same filenames and they drop straight into `index.html`.

## Tips for consistency

- If your tool takes a reference image, give it **`assets/trick-5.jpg`** as the style and
  character reference for every panel.
- Generate all five in one session, at a 1:1 aspect ratio (the originals are 1024×1024).
- Draw the **same skater** in every panel: shaggy dark hair, oversized short-sleeve tee,
  baggy cuffed pants, low-top canvas skate shoes with side stripes. (Panel 1 currently shows
  shorts. Use pants everywhere.)
- The **foot inset** (a small top-down diagram of the board in a corner) is the clearest
  way to show the variable. If the tool garbles it, drop it: the page already prints the
  variable as a handwritten note under each panel.

## Shared style block (paste at the start of every prompt)

> Graphite pencil sketch on warm cream sketchbook paper. Loose, confident linework with
> cross-hatching for shadow, soft smudged shading, visible construction lines. A teenage
> skateboarder with shaggy dark hair, oversized short-sleeve t-shirt, baggy cuffed pants,
> low-top canvas skate shoes with side stripes. Scribbled ground shadow, light pencil
> motion lines. Monochrome, no color, no lettering, no words. Storyboard / sketchbook
> illustration, single figure, plain background.

## Panel 3: `trick-3.jpg` · Attempt #3 · **bail... closer**

**Changed:** the **flick**. The front foot now drags **up and out off the toe-side nose
corner**, with the ankle rolling, instead of kicking down.

> [style block] Third kickflip attempt. The board completes a full flip and is under the
> skater, but only his front foot catches it. His back foot misses and comes down on
> the pavement. The board rolls out from under him and he is running it out on one foot,
> off balance, arms windmilling. Strong curved motion lines trace the front foot's path
> up and off the front corner of the board. Small inset sketch in the top-left corner:
> a top-down skateboard diagram showing the same angled front foot, with a bold curved

## Panel 4: `trick-4.jpg` · Attempt #4 · **almost...!**

**Changed:** the **pop**. A harder snap on the tail with knees pulled up high, so he gets
more height and time to catch.

> [style block] Fourth kickflip attempt. The skater has both feet back on the board but
> landed too far back on the tail: the nose is lifted, one front wheel is off the ground,
> knees deeply bent, arms thrown wide for balance, body wobbling and leaning back.
> Wobble lines around the board. Tense, hopeful expression. Small inset sketch in the
> top-left corner: a top-down skateboard diagram with both feet set as before, plus a
> sharp downward arrow on the tail and a small upward arrow beside the knees.

## Panel 5: `trick-5.jpg` · Attempt #5 · **LANDED!**

**Same setup, committed.** Nothing new changed. He stays over the board.

> [style block] Kickflip landed clean. The skater rolls away smoothly with both feet over
> the bolts, knees softly bent, shoulders level, arms relaxed, a small satisfied grin.
> All four wheels are on the ground, with horizontal speed lines trailing behind. No
> inset diagram, or a tiny one with a check mark next to the final foot setup.
